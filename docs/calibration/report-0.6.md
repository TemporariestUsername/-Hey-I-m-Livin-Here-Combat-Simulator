# Calibration Report 0.6

**Generated cohort:** 2026-08-02
**Engine:** 0.4.0
**Policies:** Chen-inspired 1.2.0 and sportive 1.1.0
**Design:** 64 paired deterministic seeds per canonical scenario; same scenario and seed, changing only the named target actor’s policy.

## Outcome

The automated Phase 6 calibration evidence passes its predeclared qualitative signatures and stability bands. It does not establish real-world effectiveness. Full Phase 6 closure still requires the independent human approval identified in `source-trait-review.md`.

## Paired findings

- In the open regulated duel, the Chen-inspired target selected no commitments and the sportive target averaged 11. The Chen-minus-sportive inflicted-severity difference was **-0.216170**, with a deterministic 95% paired-bootstrap interval of **[-0.231815, -0.201539]**.
- In the sudden close crisis, both policies made the same one-pulse commitment and had identical mean inflicted severity of **0.826661**. The Chen policy’s relative severity position therefore improves by 0.216170 from duel to crisis, satisfying the intended mismatch-to-crisis signature without introducing a win-rate claim.
- The effect-triggered threat-termination fixture recorded **zero** post-threat commitments under both comparison policies. The decisive effect clears the threat in the same pulse, and engine-level gate cases extend the invariant to every built-in policy.
- The one-versus-three fixture emitted exactly 12 morale signals per episode in the fixed cohort, while the four-person escort emitted a mean of 39.375 (p05 24, p95 72). Cascade depth stayed within each fixture’s configured bound.
- Across the full cohort, action distributions are distinct: the open-duel target produced 768 Chen withdrawals versus 704 sportive commitments and 64 sportive withdrawals. Crisis, escort, and crowd cases intentionally converge where the scoring order does not change.

## Coefficient dominance and provenance

All 20 distinct doctrine weight names have provenance and explanatory notes. Across 6,599 active-threat multi-term target decisions, the largest absolute single contribution share was **0.594714** (`activeThreatBase` in the Chen-inspired policy). No unexplained coefficient exists, and no single term contributes 60% or more in the audited multi-term decisions. The post-threat safety discontinuity is excluded from this balance diagnostic because it is an intentional engine rule rather than a calibrated trade-off.

## Stable regression bands

The following fixed-cohort bands are intentionally qualitative and conservative:

- Open-duel Chen-minus-sportive mean inflicted severity must remain at or below -0.15.
- Crisis Chen-minus-sportive mean inflicted severity must remain at or above -0.02, producing at least +0.13 relative improvement from the duel result.
- Open-duel action totals must differ by at least 512 decisions.
- Every one-versus-three cohort episode must emit at least one morale signal and no depth may exceed the configured cap.
- Post-threat commitment means must remain exactly zero in every canonical comparison.
- Favorable-factor sensitivity lower confidence bounds must remain non-negative.
- The maximum active-threat multi-term contribution share must remain below 0.60, and unexplained weight count must remain zero.

## Assumptions and limitations

The policies are synthetic, the six scenarios are small abstractions, actors have access only to generated observations, and confidence intervals cover seed variability rather than uncertainty in the model structure. Schema 1.3 models ambient noise, hearing, attention, memory, light, cover, and occlusion as bounded game abstractions; none is empirical human-sensing evidence. No result predicts injury, survival, legality, psychology, or the real behavior of any martial tradition.

The authoritative machine-readable evidence is `canonical-results.json`, generated by `npm run calibrate`. Regeneration is deterministic and the verifier checks the fixture hashes, policy versions, sample count, bands, and report contract.
